## A. Phone business vs. the wider Nokia corporation

The script depends on this distinction. Nokia Corporation was (and is) a telecommunications company. The mobile-phone business ("Devices & Services") was its largest division for years, but it was one division. What Microsoft bought in 2014 was that division and a licence to Nokia's patents (F28). Nokia retained its network-equipment business and, until 2015, its HERE maps unit (F29, F31). That is why Nokia can be both "a company that lost the smartphone war" and "a company that still exists, with ~€20bn in revenue" (F32).

## C. Facts vs. interpretations (as presented in the film)

| Statement | Status |
|---|---|
| Share, shipment and sale figures; dates of launches and deals | **Fact** (with the caveats above) |
| Symbian's technical limits; partners leaving | **Fact** (sourced, though from a compiled article) |
| Elop's memo wording and his stated reasons for Windows Phone | **Fact** (what was said), not proof that the reasoning was right |
| "Nokia was in crisis before the numbers showed it" (Doz & Wilson) | **Interpretation** — a scholarly argument |
| Fear distorted upward information flow (Vuori & Huy) | **Interpretation** — qualitative research on 2005–2010 interviews |
| Windows Phone announcement caused the sales collapse ("Osborne effect") | **Interpretation / contested** — the film presents both sides |
| "Success made bad news expensive"; "every response has a cost" | **The channel's own conclusions** — labelled |

**What the film deliberately does *not* claim:**
- No boardroom conversations are reconstructed.
- It does not say one decision "killed" Nokia.
- It does not say Android would definitely have saved Nokia (that is an unfalsifiable counterfactual).
- It does not use claims I could not confirm: a 2012 net-loss figure of €2.3bn (not found), a Q3 2010 smartphone figure of 26.5M (not found), and the full-year 2011 loss (sources differ between €1.07bn and €1.2bn — the script avoids it).
