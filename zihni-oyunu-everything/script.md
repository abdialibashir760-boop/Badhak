# Zihni Oyunu — The Psychology of People Who Want to Do Everything

**Working title:** Why You Want to Do Everything (And Finish Nothing)
**Alternative title:** The Weight of Every Possible Life
**Target length:** about 5–6 minutes of narration (~820 words)

## Narration

You have a list.

Not a short one.

Learn a language. Start a business. Get fit. Read more. Write the book. Learn guitar. Travel. Fix your sleep. Call your friends more often.

Every item feels important.

Every item feels urgent.

And every morning, you wake up already behind.

So you start.

You buy the course. You download the app. You make a new plan, and for a few days, you feel alive.

Then something new appears.

Another idea. Another opportunity. Another version of you that you could become.

And the old plan quietly joins the others.

Unfinished.

If this sounds familiar, you are not lazy.

In many cases, it is the opposite.

You care about too many things at once.

But why does the mind want everything?

For some people, every choice feels like a loss.

If you choose one path, you close another.

If you commit to one goal, you may have to accept that other goals will wait. Maybe for years. Maybe forever.

And that can feel unbearable.

So instead of choosing, you try to keep every door open.

It feels like freedom.

But holding every door open takes both hands.

And then you cannot walk through any of them.

There is another layer.

For some people, doing more becomes a way of feeling worthy.

Productivity starts to sound like proof.

If I am busy, I am useful.

If I am improving, I am enough.

If I stop, I might fall behind.

So rest begins to feel like failure.

And an empty afternoon feels less like peace and more like a threat.

Then there is the world around you.

Every day, you see people who seem to be doing everything.

Someone is training for a marathon. Someone is launching a company. Someone is learning their third language.

You do not see their tired evenings.

You do not see what they gave up.

You only see the highlight, and you compare it to your entire life.

So your list grows.

Not because you truly want everything on it, but because you are afraid of being the one who did less.

And when everything matters, a strange thing happens.

Nothing gets your full attention.

Your energy is divided into tiny pieces.

You make a little progress in many directions, and almost none in one.

The beginning of something new feels exciting.

The middle feels slow, uncertain, and ordinary.

So you leave in the middle.

Again and again.

Over time, you may start to believe something painful about yourself.

That you never finish anything.

That you are not disciplined.

That something is wrong with you.

But the problem may not be your character.

It may be that you are trying to carry more than one life at a time.

Now imagine a different approach.

Not doing nothing.

Doing less, on purpose.

Imagine looking at your list and asking one honest question.

What matters most in this season of my life?

Not forever.

Just now.

Choosing one thing does not mean the others are worthless.

It means they are waiting.

A goal you postpone is not a goal you have abandoned.

It is simply not your turn yet.

And when you choose, you will probably feel discomfort.

You may feel fear.

What if I picked the wrong thing?

What if I miss something better?

That feeling is normal.

Every real choice carries the loss of the paths you did not take.

You cannot avoid that loss by refusing to choose.

You only delay it, while your energy drains away.

So you choose.

You give one thing your attention.

You let it become boring sometimes.

You stay through the middle.

And slowly, something changes.

You begin to trust yourself again.

Not because you did everything.

Because you finished something.

Rest stops feeling like failure.

It becomes part of the work.

Other people's lives stop looking like a list of tasks you are failing to complete.

They are simply different lives.

You are allowed to want many things.

Curiosity is not a flaw.

Ambition is not a flaw.

But you are one person, with one body, one mind, and a limited amount of time.

That is not a weakness.

It is what makes your choices meaningful.

So the next time you feel the urge to start everything at once, pause.

Look at your list.

And ask yourself:

If I could only give my full attention to one thing right now, what would it be?

Then begin there.

Because a meaningful life is not built by doing everything.

It is built by choosing what deserves your time, and letting the rest wait.

## Visual ideas (same stickman and style as the first film)
- **The list:** a paper list unrolls from the ceiling and keeps growing until it buries the floor.
- **The doors:** a corridor of doors, the character stretched between them, one hand on each handle.
- **The lights:** tiny progress bars above many projects, each stuck at 10–20%.
- **Comparison:** silhouettes in the distance doing impressive things; when the camera moves closer, the hidden cost appears behind them.
- **The middle:** a path that starts bright and becomes grey; the character turns back each time.
- **The choice:** the character closes all doors but one, gently, without locking them; the list rolls back up and leaves one line.
- **Ending:** a single finished thing (a small plant grown from a seed) and calm open space.
